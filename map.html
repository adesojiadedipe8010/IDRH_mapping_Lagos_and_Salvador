Spatial Narratives of Division

The comparative study of Lagos and Salvador reveals striking parallels in how urban infrastructure has been used to manage, relocate, or exclude specific populations. In Lagos, the Lagos Executive Development Board (LEDB) acquired Victoria Island in 1948, solidifying it as an elite enclave far removed from the mainland density.

In Salvador, the construction of Avenida Lafayete Coutinho in 1962 serves as a physical manifestation of segregation, severing the working-class coastal community of Gamboa de Baixo from the upper city center. Similarly, both cities have seen "gentrification" through international recognition—exemplified by the displacement of Afro-Brazilians in Pelourinho after its UNESCO designation.
